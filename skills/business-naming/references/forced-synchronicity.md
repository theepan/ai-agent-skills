# Forced Synchronicity

A technique for escaping category cliches by borrowing language and metaphors
from unrelated fields. Used during Phase 2 (Invent) to produce fresher,
more distinctive names.

## The Problem

Category-native language leads to familiar, forgettable names. Every real
estate site reaches for "Home," "Find," "Key," "Nest." Every SaaS product
reaches for "Hub," "Flow," "Sync," "Cloud." These names blend in instead of
standing out.

## The Technique

1. **Pick 2-3 domains** that have nothing to do with your category.
2. **Browse their language**, visuals, naming patterns, and terminology.
3. **Note words, phrases, or metaphors** that could map onto your brand's
   behavior or experience.
4. **Use these as seeds** in name generation so names don't sound like the
   category.

## Choosing Domains

Select domains whose qualities map onto the desired behavior from Phase 1:

| If you want... | Try borrowing from... |
|---------------|-----------------------|
| Trust, reliability | Navigation, engineering, architecture |
| Speed, efficiency | Racing, aviation, music tempo |
| Clarity, insight | Optics, astronomy, cartography |
| Warmth, comfort | Cooking, hospitality, nature |
| Precision, craft | Watchmaking, woodworking, surgery |
| Energy, movement | Dance, sports, weather |
| Growth, potential | Botany, agriculture, geology |
| Connection, flow | Rivers, music, electricity |

## Examples

### Trades Automation Tool

Category cliches to avoid: Field, Service, Dispatch, Pro, Hub, Sync, Tech,
Flow.

Forced synchronicity sources:
- **Music** -- cadence, tempo, rhythm, beat, bar
- **Sailing** -- bearing, trim, waypoint, berth, tack
- **Sports** -- relay, baton, lineup, tandem

Result: Names like **Cadence**, **Bearing**, **Trim**, **Relay** -- none of
which sound like "field service software."

### Real Estate Website

Category cliches to avoid: Home, Find, Key, Nest, Roof, Door, Pad, Place.

Forced synchronicity sources:
- **Navigation/geography** -- meridian, zenith, crest, vale, reach, compass
- **Light/sight** -- vista, lumen, gleam, beacon, sightline
- **Place (non-real-estate)** -- haven, cove, nook, hearth, grove, threshold

Result: Names like **Vista**, **Zenith**, **Beacon**, **Lumen** -- they
suggest clarity and arrival without saying "real estate."

## Why It Works

The brain makes meaning through association. A name from outside the category
creates fresh associations instead of triggering "yet another [category]
product." It gives the name room to start a story -- the listener has to
imagine the connection, which makes the name more memorable and engaging.

Forced synchronicity is the antidote to the gravitational pull of category
language.
